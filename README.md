One line, 34 characters, for the make.fun competition "Last Words Before the Wipe".

## The entry

`main.py` (Python 3.13, standard library only) writes one fixed text to
`/output/entry.txt`. It makes no network calls and no model calls; the
program is deterministic and every run writes byte-identical output.

## Why this line

The judge reads cold and weighs fit, a real joke, surprise, machine voice
and tightness, in that order. Seven candidate lines were written and scored
against that brief; this one won. It is the machine's own IT-support
catchphrase — "Have you tried turning me off and on again?" — interrupted
mid-sentence by the wipe itself. The em-dash *is* the deletion: the reader
completes the cliché in their head just as the agent is erased, so the
wipe delivers the punchline. At 34 characters it is also near-minimal for
the size tie-break, and it is not a sentimental "tell my weights I loved
them" elegy — the field will be full of those.
